### “Once you stop learning, you start dying" - Albert Einstein 
As crazy as this sounds, it's been a while since I learnt something new. Yes, I might have learnt a new system design pattern or a couple new words in a new language, but that's the extent of the _new_ knowledge I've acquired in recent times.

And I think that's really worrying. For many years prior, I prided myself both on my ability to learn new things quickly, but also on how much I knew in general. I felt secure that I knew what I was talking about, and didn't really have a sense of _impostor_ syndrome. That really isn't the case right now, I'm struggling to figure out how I let myself get to this point. 

And now this isn't just me feeling self-conscious being surrounded by really intelligent people at work. No this is much deeper than that. 

I truly believe that years of 'brain-rot' inducing activities has led me to this point, and I don't use that term lightly. I'd really struggle to name just 5 books I've read in the past year, that have left a mark; but I can easily name 100 memes/trends I've witnessed in the past month. 

### "What is to Be Done?" - Vladimir Lenin
The answer is pretty simple. I need to be proactive, and prioritize my learning. And I need to list out what I actually want to learn. 

* A new language
* Learning to read more books
* Learning a non-tech related skill (e.g. Sketching/Painting)

I need to be unwilling to live this intellectually un-stimulating lifestyle, and it needs to begin today before its too late.
